Safeguarding Policy

Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy

ISK is committed to the protection and welfare of every child. This policy outlines our safeguarding framework, reporting procedures, and the responsibilities of every member of our school community.

Introduction

A safe environment for every child.

The International School of Kuantan Board of Governors, in keeping with the ISK core values and vision statements, has adopted a Child Protection Policy to guide our staff and families in matters related to the protection and safeguarding of all children. All adults within the ISK community should make the protection and welfare of children a priority.

This policy provides the school with the basis for good practice and a framework to ensure consistency in matters related to child safeguarding and protection. The school's policy abides by the rationale that guides Malaysia's policies and service provisions for children and young persons, including the Malaysian Child Act 2001 and the United Nation's Convention on the Rights of the Child.

Core Principles

Our guiding values for child safety.

Paramount Importance

The school's responsibility to safeguard and promote the welfare of children is of paramount importance in everything we do.

Equal Protection

All children, regardless of age, gender, ability, culture, race, language, religion or sexual identity, have equal rights to protection.

Safe Learning

Children who are safe and feel safe are better equipped to learn. This school is committed to safeguarding and promoting the welfare of all children.

Shared Responsibility

All staff have an equal responsibility to act on any suspicion or disclosure that may suggest a child is at risk of harm at home, in the community or in school.

Vigilance

All staff members will maintain an attitude of “It could happen here” where safeguarding is concerned, always acting in the best interest of the child.

Immediate Action

If at any point there is a risk of immediate serious harm to a child, a referral will be made to the Child Protection Service and the relevant authorities immediately.

Safeguarding Team

Designated Safeguarding & Protection Team

The ISK Student Support Team (SST) is tasked with Child Safeguarding and Protection. All members of the school community are made aware of who the SST members are and their roles.

SST Head

Safeguarding Lead

First point of contact for reporting suspicions or concerns. Ensures proper procedures are followed, coordinates action, provides staff training, and keeps detailed confidential records.

SST Members

Deputy Safeguarding Leads

Serve as backup if the Safeguarding Lead is unavailable. Assist with filing confidential records and reports, and support safeguarding responsibilities as required.

Support

School Counselor / Principal

Provides support and reassurance to children identified as requiring protection. Alerts to specific needs of children in need and those with special education needs.

ISK's Principal is responsible for the implementation of this policy and oversees all safeguarding referral decisions in consultation with the Safeguarding Lead and the Board of Governors.

Safeguarding Measures

How we protect our students every day.

Staff Screening

All staff are subject to reference and background checks in accordance with Malaysian labor laws. Overseas recruitment uses only established agencies with appropriate vetting procedures.

Code of Conduct

Standards of behavior are outlined in the Employee Handbook. All staff must sign an acknowledgement of the Staff Code of Conduct as a binding part of their contract.

Volunteers & Vendors

Volunteers and contracted service employees must work under direct staff supervision and will never be given sole charge of any child. Vendor work is scheduled after school hours when possible.

Confidentiality

All matters relating to child protection are confidential, disclosed on a need-to-know basis only. Informer identities are protected under the Malaysian Child Act 2001.

Training & Education

Staff receive annual child safeguarding training. The school provides opportunities and resources for students to learn about personal safety, including keeping safe online.

Mobile Phones & Media

Strict policies govern mobile phone usage, photography, and social media. Children's full names are never combined with their images in public-facing materials.

Awareness

Recognising the signs of abuse.

Though the signs listed below do not necessarily indicate abuse, they may help adults recognise that something is wrong. The possibility of abuse should be considered if a child shows a number of these symptoms or any of them to a marked degree.

4.1.1

Neglect

Failure to provide for the development of the child in basic areas including health, education, emotional development, nutrition, shelter, and safe living conditions.

Possible signs include: Untreated medical problems, constant hunger, poor personal hygiene, fatigue, poor school attendance, low self-esteem, compulsive stealing, extreme need for affection, or failure to achieve developmental milestones.

4.1.2

Physical Abuse

Inflicting physical injury on a child by other than accidental means, including skin bruising, burns, disfigurement, and loss or impairment of any bodily function.

Possible signs include: Unexplained recurrent injuries, refusal to discuss injuries, improbable explanations, self-destructive tendencies, aggression, fear of physical contact, or fear of returning home.

4.1.3

Emotional Abuse

Persistent emotional maltreatment that causes severe and persistent adverse effects on the child's emotional and behavioural development.

Possible signs include: Delayed development, sudden speech disorders, low self-esteem, extreme fear of new situations, over-reaction to mistakes, extreme passivity or aggression, or sudden under-achievement.

4.1.4

Sexual Abuse

Forcing or enticing a child to take part in contact or non-contact sexual activities, whether or not the child comprehends or is aware of what is happening.

Possible signs include: Age-inappropriate sexual knowledge, personality changes, sexually explicit language, regression to younger behaviour, substance abuse, self-harm, eating disorders, or fear of being left alone with specific individuals.

Reporting Procedure

Steps for dealing with suspected abuse.

An ISK employee's role is not to investigate or verify suspicion, but rather to report to the designated Safeguarding Lead and set in motion the process of getting help for the child.

1
Report Immediately

Report concerns and any evidence to the designated Safeguarding Lead as soon as possible. If a child disclosed to you directly, make a record of the conversation using their own words.

2
Safeguarding Lead Reviews

The Safeguarding Lead immediately collects and reviews evidence with SST members. If there are obvious signs of harm, the child is taken for medical attention.

3
Child Interview

If no immediate medical needs, the Safeguarding Lead and School Counselor communicate with the child directly. A summary report is documented on the Child Protection Concern form.

4
Evaluate & Decide

The Safeguarding Lead meets with the Principal and at least one Board of Governors member to evaluate all evidence, discuss appropriate action, and/or make a referral decision.

5
External Referral (if needed)

If needed, referral is made to the Child Protection Service, police, or other agencies by the Principal in consultation with the Board of Governors.

Staff Guidelines

How to respond if a child discloses abuse.

  1. Ask the child if they would prefer to speak to a counselor.
  2. Reassure the child that it is okay to tell what happened and that whatever happened is not their fault.
  3. Never promise the child confidentiality or that you will “keep this a secret.”
  4. Project a calm, understanding, and supportive attitude — it may be traumatic for the child to recall events.
  5. Never jump to conclusions or react with shock, anger, or horror.
  6. Never speculate or confront another person allegedly involved.
  7. Use open questions such as “Tell me”, “Describe to me”, or “Explain to me.”
  8. Listen carefully without judgement. Let the child tell you what they want and no more.
  9. Make a careful record using the child's own words. Never ask the child to sign the record.
  10. After reporting, maintain a supportive presence for the child.

Physical Environment

A campus designed for safety.

Glass Door Inserts

All classroom doors have clear glass windows so activity can be monitored. No decorations or coverings may obstruct the view.

CCTV Surveillance

Strategically placed throughout the school except in restrooms and changing rooms. Access to recordings is restricted to authorised staff only.

Well-Lit Spaces

Hallways and classrooms are well-lit. Staff should not be alone with a child in areas that are not well-lit. Classrooms are only locked when not in use.
